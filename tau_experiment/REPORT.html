Discrete causal bottleneck · straight-through temperature

Annealing the Causal Bottleneck

The textbook temperature schedule is backwards here. Reversing it recovers causal states the pipeline was merging — but only on the runs that were already failing.

320 trained models 8 processes × directions 13 phase-cells 6 phases 150 epochs each CPU, exactly repeatable

Recommended

tau = geom:0.5:5  ·  betas = 0.9, 0.999

Rise τ geometrically from ~0.5 to ~5 across training. Leave Adam alone — no momentum setting beat its default. Expect this to matter only where the bottleneck is currently merging states.

The caveat that shapes everything below

Averaged over the four cells the winner did not choose, the schedule bought nothing: −0.036 ± 0.049 bits, and the phase-1B/1C winner was marginally worse than the pipeline. The gains are real but conditional.

The finding

A rescue, not a speed-up

Across all 13 phase-cells (8 distinct processes × directions, some measured in more than one phase), how much a rising schedule helps is predicted by how badly the pipeline was already doing. Where const:1 already lands within ~0.3 bits of the closed-form complexity, there is no headroom and the schedule changes nothing. Where it fails, the schedule recovers most of the gap.

Which schedule, and how much it matters

Mechanism

Why the direction is what matters

τ never changes what the model computes. The forward value is hard = one_hot(argmax(z)) at every τ, because argmax is scale-invariant. τ only shapes the straight-through gradient:

∂pi/∂zj = (1/τ)·pi(δij − pj)

Two consequences drive everything. The off-diagonal mass decays like e−Δ/τ, so a state that has already lost gets exponentially little gradient — a lockout. And the whole bottleneck gradient carries a 1/τ factor, including the β·H(p̄) usage penalty, which minimises occupancy entropy and so actively prunes states.

Both bite late, because both depend on a logit gap that training itself creates. Low τ is nearly free early and expensive late; high τ is uninformative early and protective late. The schedule that matches is low → high.

Momentum: a clean negative

The hypothesis was that Adam's momenta attack the same gradient starvation from the other side — higher β₁ so a rarely-updated state still accumulates, lower β₂ so its normaliser re-adapts faster. Both predictions were wrong.

The one nominally better arm sits well inside its own noise at a 33% win rate. Once τ is scheduled, |S−C| is already ~0.10 and there is nothing left for momentum to fix.

Limits

What this does not establish

  • Two seeds per cell in phases 1B–1D. Only the family distinction (rising vs constant vs falling) clears two SEM; no individual rising schedule is separable from another.
  • The winner does not transfer. 1D is the check that matters and it came back flat. Treat the schedule as a fix for cells that are visibly merging states, not as a new default to apply blind.
  • No cell reaches its true state count on the hard end. flower(2,8) backward has true k = 9 and the best arm recovers a fraction of that. The ceiling documented in DISCRETE_STATE_EXPERIMENTS.md §6.10 is raised, not removed.
  • One cell was salvaged from its log after being killed at 20 of 24 runs. All 20 are genuine; the JSON is marked PARTIAL and its arms carry n = 1 rather than n = 2 where the job stopped.
  • The 14-cell pooled ranking is confounded and is not used here. Arms ran on different cell sets, so pooling across phases partly measures which cells an arm saw. Only the per-phase balanced tables above are valid.

Where everything lives

Nothing in Experimental_pipeline/ was modified. τ is a plain attribute read fresh on every forward, and Adam's betas live in param_groups, so both are injected from outside the model class.

An arm is fully described by the string that produced it — geom:0.5:5, hold:0.5:5:0.3, adam_b1:0.5:0.99 — and that same token appears in the command line, the JSON and the figure legend. Every figure redraws from saved JSON without retraining: python analyse.py.

Runs on CPU for exact repeatability (MPS is not reproducible — training.py:284). Data, split and initialisation are pure functions of the seed, which is what makes arms from separate invocations pair run for run.
